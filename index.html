What use is microeconomic theory?

Part I

Why Does Economic Theory Matter?

Economic theory is often questioned for its distance from the real world. Varian identifies several concrete reasons why theory remains an indispensable tool — not just as an intellectual exercise, but as a practical instrument for analysis and policy.

Substituting for Data

When direct empirical data is unavailable or costly to gather, theory allows economists to predict outcomes by reasoning from first principles and assumptions.

Guiding the Identification of Key Parameters

Theory tells us which variables and parameters are actually important for understanding a given economic phenomenon — saving researchers from collecting irrelevant information.

Connecting Disparate Problems

Theory reveals that seemingly unrelated economic problems share a common structure. A single framework can illuminate labour markets, insurance, taxation, and auctions all at once.

Generating Useful Insights

Even an imperfect or simplified model can produce valuable qualitative insights into the mechanisms at work — insights that feed directly into policy debates, even when precise quantification is impossible.

Offering Quantification & Calculation

Theory provides a rigorous framework for measuring relationships between variables — elasticities, welfare gains, deadweight losses — that are essential inputs for informed policy decision-making.

Serving as an Antidote to Introspection

Personal experience and intuition systematically mislead us. Theory imposes discipline and objectivity, forcing economists to check their reasoning against a coherent logical structure rather than gut feeling.

Enabling Experimental Verification

Theory produces testable hypotheses. By formalising propositions that can be confronted with data or laboratory experiments, theory creates a cumulative, self-correcting discipline: experiments refine theories, and refined theories guide new experiments.

Part II

The Dilemmas of an Economic Theorist

Presenting economic theory is not without tension. Rubinstein identifies four recurring challenges — or dilemmas — that every economic theorist must confront when defending or communicating their work.

Theory leads to absurd conclusions Theory is rejected by the data Empirics alone identify regularities Theory is irrelevant to the real world
1
The Dilemma of Absurd Conclusions

What if a model predicts something obviously wrong?

Challenge

Should a model be discarded entirely when it produces results that seem absurd or counterfactual?

Response

All models are deliberate simplifications of reality — they will inevitably fail in some contexts. Absurd conclusions are not a reason to discard a model; they are a signal. They reveal the limits of the model's assumptions and create opportunities to reinterpret them or build better ones.

2
The Dilemma of Responding to Evidence

What if the data contradict the model?

Challenge

Should economic models be adjusted or outright rejected when contradicted by experimental or empirical evidence?

Response

Empirical refutations should be treated as inputs for refinement, not grounds for dismissal. Economists should use contradicting evidence as a basis for improving existing models or developing entirely new ones that better capture observed behaviour.

3
The Dilemma of Modelless Regularities

What if patterns can be found without any model?

Challenge

Should economists need formal models to discover regularities in human behaviour, or can direct observation do the job just as well?

Response

Regularities can indeed be observed directly without theory. However, models remain valuable even then: they provide a structured, systematic framework for thinking about those regularities, making them communicable, generalisable, and testable beyond the original context.

4
The Dilemma of Relevance

Is theory too detached from the real world to matter?

Challenge

Can economic theorists offer meaningful, actionable advice, or are their models inevitably too abstract and detached to be relevant to real-world policy?

Response

Theory may not always produce ready-made policy prescriptions — but it is still fundamentally relevant because it shapes how people think about economic problems. Theorists carry a responsibility: they must present their work honestly, acknowledge their models' limitations, and resist oversimplification when confronting complex real-world challenges.

Key Takeaway

Economic theory is not a perfect description of the world. Its value lies in structuring thought, identifying what matters, connecting problems, and generating testable predictions — while theorists must remain honest about its limits.
