What Use Is Microeconomic Theory?

Part I
Varian's Advocacy
Discussion Question
According to you, what use is economic theory?
📊

1. Substituting for Data

Some situations can never be directly observed — you cannot rerun history or collect data on a policy that was never enacted. Theory lets you reason from assumptions to predict what would have happened, filling the gap that data cannot fill. For example: what would employment look like if the minimum wage had never existed? No data can answer that. A model can.

🎯

2. Guiding the Identification of Key Parameters

Without theory, a researcher doesn't know where to look. Theory acts as a compass: it tells you that, for this specific problem, what really matters is just one or two key parameters — for instance, the price elasticity of demand, or the elasticity of substitution between labour and capital. You focus your data collection there, and ignore the rest.

🔗

3. Connecting Disparate Problems

Theory reveals that two problems that look completely different are actually the same problem in disguise. The decision of how much insurance to buy and the effort a worker puts in when unmonitored both share the same structure: moral hazard. Once you understand one, you understand both — and the policy solutions transfer directly.

💡

4. Generating Useful Insights

A model doesn't have to be perfectly realistic to be useful. Even the simple model of perfect competition — which no real market perfectly matches — immediately tells you: a monopolist charges too high a price and produces too little. That qualitative insight directly informs antitrust policy, even without precise numbers.

🧮

5. Offering Quantification and Calculation

Verbal reasoning tells you "a tax reduces economic activity." But policymakers need to know by how much. Theory lets you calculate exact magnitudes — the deadweight loss of a tax, the consumer surplus gained by a subsidy, the optimal tariff rate. Without that, cost-benefit analysis is impossible.

🧠

6. Serving as an Antidote to Introspection

Personal intuition misleads. Your gut feeling might say "more immigration means lower wages for local workers, because labour supply increases." Theory shows the answer actually depends on whether immigrants and locals are substitutes or complements in production — something introspection alone will never reveal. Theory forces you to think more carefully than your instincts allow.

🔬

7. Enabling Experimental Verification

Theory produces precise, testable predictions. For instance, competitive market theory predicts that prices should equal marginal cost. That's a clear claim you can go and verify in the data. If the prediction fails, you know exactly which assumption to revise. This feedback loop between theory and experiment is what makes economics a cumulative, self-correcting science.

Part II
Rubinstein's Dilemmas
Discussion Question
Put yourself in the shoes of an economic theorist: what challenges do you face when presenting your work?
• Theory leads to absurd conclusions • Theory is rejected by the data • Empirics are enough to identify behavioral regularities • Theory is not relevant to the real-world issues
1
The Dilemma of Absurd Conclusions
❓ Question / Challenge

Should a model be discarded if it leads to absurd conclusions, or should it be seen as a limited tool that fails in some contexts?

✔ Answer & Resolution

Economists should recognize that all models are simplifications of reality and will inevitably fail in some contexts. These absurdities highlight the limitations of the model's assumptions and serve as an opportunity to reinterpret them.

2
The Dilemma of Responding to Evidence
❓ Question / Challenge

Should economic models be adjusted or rejected if they are contradicted by experimental evidence?

✔ Answer & Resolution

Economists should use empirical refutations as a basis for refining models or developing new ones. When real-world evidence contradicts a model, it shows us where the model needs improvement.

3
The Dilemma of Modelless Regularities
❓ Question / Challenge

Should economic theorists rely on models to discover regularities in human behavior, or can these regularities be identified directly through observation?

✔ Answer & Resolution

Regularities can often be observed directly without the need for models. However, models can still play a useful role by providing a structured way to think about these regularities.

4
The Dilemma of Relevance
❓ Question / Challenge

Can economic theorists offer meaningful advice that impacts the real world, or are their models too detached from reality to be relevant?

✔ Answer & Resolution

While economic theory may not always provide actionable advice, it is still relevant because it shapes the way people think about economic issues. Theorists should be responsible in how they present their work, recognizing the limitations of their models and avoiding oversimplification when addressing complex real-world problems.
